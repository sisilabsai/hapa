User Registration
& Onboarding

The first thirty seconds of Hapa must feel like the platform already knows what it's doing. Registration is not a form — it is Hapa's first act of intelligence. Location before identity. Context before data.

"The first thing Hapa asks is not your name or your email. It asks where you are. Because that is the only question that matters."

// Design Principle — Registration
1.1 — Registration Flow
STEP 01 📍

Location First

The app opens to a single screen: a pulsing location pin over a softly blurred map of the user's detected city. One line of text: "Hapa is live in [City Name]." One button: "I'm here."

Location permission is requested at this exact moment — before any sign-up form. Users who grant permission see their city animate into view. Users who decline are shown a manual city search. Location is never forced, but the experience is clearly richer with it. The emotional message: Hapa already knows where you are. It is ready for you.
GPS detectionManual fallbackCity-level only at this stage
STEP 02 🔑

Quick Account Creation

After location, account creation is one screen. Phone number preferred — because in African markets, phone numbers are primary identity. Email optional. Social login (Google) available as an alternative path.

Phone number → OTP verification → done. No email confirmation loops. No password complexity requirements at this stage. The goal is to be inside the app within 90 seconds of first open. Friction here is a product failure.
Phone + OTPGoogle OAuthEmail optionalNo passwords required
STEP 03 🧩

Identity Setup — Who Are You Here?

A minimal profile setup screen. Name, optional photo, and one critical question: "How would you describe yourself in this city?" Four tile options — resident, new arrival, traveller passing through, or business owner. This single choice shapes the entire first-session experience.

This is not demographic collection. It is context collection. A "new arrival" gets cultural onboarding content surfaced immediately. A "resident" gets local trending content. A "traveller" gets survival-layer recommendations. A "business owner" gets directed toward Hapa Business. Each path is meaningfully different from the first tap.
ResidentNew ArrivalTravellerBusiness Owner
STEP 04 🎯

Interest Selection — What Do You Care About?

A visual tile grid of interest categories. Users pick three to seven. These seed the recommendation engine immediately — no cold start. The feed on day one already feels relevant.

Categories: Food & Dining · Nightlife & Entertainment · Arts & Culture · Business & Networking · Sports & Fitness · Faith & Community · Family & Parenting · Fashion & Style · Music & Events · Street Life · Travel · Tech · Nature & Outdoors. These are not permanent — Hapa learns and adjusts them continuously based on behaviour.
Visual tile grid3–7 selectionsSeeds recommendation engineAdjustable anytime
STEP 05 🌍

Language Preference

Select primary language for content and AI responses. English is default. Local languages available per city: Luganda (Kampala), Swahili (Nairobi / Dar), Yoruba (Lagos), Twi (Accra), Amharic (Addis). More languages unlock as Hapa expands.

Language preference affects: AI guide responses, community content priority, creator discovery, business listing descriptions. A Luganda speaker in Kampala sees a Kampala experience that feels genuinely local — not a translated version of a Western product.
EnglishLugandaSwahiliYorubaTwiAmharic
STEP 06 🏠

First Feed — Hapa Is Alive

Registration complete. No tutorial overlay. No empty state. The user lands directly in a populated, location-relevant feed. For day-one cities, content is seeded by founding local creators. For new cities, Hapa Guide content loads as the first experience.

The principle: never show an empty app. The first feed must feel alive, local, and worth scrolling. Hapa's product quality is judged in this first scroll. Every founding city launch includes a minimum of 200 seeded posts before public opening.
Pre-seeded contentNo empty statesImmediate relevance

Business
Onboarding

A mama mboga in Kampala and a hotel GM in Nairobi must both be able to register their business without calling support. The target: live on Hapa in under 10 minutes. Any more friction and we lose them.

"Free to start. Live in 10 minutes. Paid when they see value. That is the only business onboarding philosophy that works in emerging markets."

// Design Principle — Business Onboarding
2.1 — Business Registration Flow
STEP 01🗂️

Category Selection

Visual tile grid — not a dropdown. Business owner taps their category. Simple, fast, no ambiguity.

Primary categories: Food & Dining · Accommodation · Transport & Rides · Health & Wellness · Beauty & Grooming · Retail & Shopping · Entertainment & Nightlife · Professional Services · Education · Events & Venues · Arts & Culture · Faith & Community · Technology · Agriculture & Markets. Sub-categories load after primary selection for precision.
Visual tilesPrimary + sub-categoryMulti-category supported
STEP 02📌

Location Pinning

The business pins their exact location on a map. Auto-detected GPS as starting point, draggable to precise spot. Street address field optional — many informal businesses don't have formal addresses. The pin is the address.

This is critical infrastructure. Hapa's entire recommendation engine depends on accurate business locations. Businesses with imprecise pins surface less frequently. The UI makes this clear — accuracy is rewarded with visibility.
GPS auto-detectDrag to pinNo formal address requiredWhat3Words integration optional
STEP 03📝

Basic Profile — Minimum Viable Listing

Five fields. That's it. Business name, one cover photo, one sentence description (120 characters), phone number, and opening hours. Submit and go live. Everything else is optional enrichment.

The minimum viable listing philosophy: it is better to have 1,000 businesses with basic profiles than 200 businesses with perfect ones. We lower the floor dramatically. Businesses can enrich their profiles at any time. The first priority is getting them on the map.
5 required fieldsCover photo120-char descriptionOpening hours
STEP 04✅

Verification

Not a document check — a human confirmation. Hapa sends a WhatsApp message or phone call to the registered number. Business owner confirms they are real. Verified badge applied within minutes.

Why WhatsApp? Because in East and West Africa, WhatsApp is primary business communication. Every business owner has it. Email verification loops are a Western assumption. Hapa meets businesses where they already are. For larger businesses (hotels, chains), a secondary document verification tier is available for an enhanced trust badge.
WhatsApp confirmationPhone call fallbackEnhanced tier for chainsMinutes not days
STEP 05🎯

Tier Selection — Free First

After verification, a simple screen explains what the free tier gives them. No credit card. No trial period. They are live. An upgrade prompt appears after 30 days — once they have seen the value.

Free tier: basic listing, location pin, standard search visibility, review collection, phone/WhatsApp contact button. Paid tiers add: priority placement in searches, Hapa Boost access (push promotions), booking integration, analytics dashboard, creator partnership eligibility, multiple photos, menu or service listing, staff profiles.
Free to startNo card requiredUpgrade after 30 daysValue first

The Creator
System

Hapa Voices is the local creator layer. It exists to solve the single biggest failure of the African creator economy: audience reach without local impact. On Hapa, a creator's audience actually shows up.

"A food creator in Lagos with 50,000 local followers is worth more to a restaurant than a global creator with 5 million followers who are mostly abroad. Hapa is the first platform that values local depth over global breadth."

// Design Principle — Creator System
3.1 — Becoming a Creator on Hapa
STEP 01✍️

Creator Application

Any user with a verified account can apply to become a Hapa Creator. The application asks three things: what city are you based in, what is your content focus, and submit three examples of content you'd post on Hapa.

No follower threshold to apply. Hapa is not looking for influencers with big existing audiences — it is looking for genuinely knowledgeable locals. A boda driver who knows every shortcut in Kampala is a more valuable Hapa Creator than a micro-celebrity with 10,000 followers who never leaves their apartment.
Open applicationNo minimum followersContent examples requiredCity-specific
STEP 02🔍

Review & Verification

Hapa's local community team reviews applications within 48 hours. They are looking for: genuine local knowledge, authentic voice, and content that serves the Hapa community rather than just promoting products.

The review is human-first, not algorithmic. In each launch city, Hapa maintains a small local team of 2–3 community managers who know the city deeply. They are the judges of what constitutes genuine local knowledge. This is a quality moat that no algorithm can replicate.
Human review48-hour turnaroundLocal team judges
STEP 03🏅

Creator Tier Assignment

Approved creators are placed on one of three tiers based on their content quality, local credibility, and community contribution. Tiers determine content reach, monetisation access, and platform support level.

Rising Voice — New creators, building audience. Access to basic creator tools, standard reach, creator community access. Local Voice — Established creators with strong local engagement. Priority feed placement, Hapa Boost co-funding, business partnership eligibility. City Voice — Hapa's top creators per city. Featured placement, creator fund access, city ambassador status, media opportunities.
Rising VoiceLocal VoiceCity Voice

The fundamental unit of Hapa content. A photo, short video (up to 60s), or text update tagged to an exact location. "I'm at this spot right now. Here's what it's like." Visible to everyone within a configurable radius and all followers.

"The vibes at Jazz Corner tonight are immaculate" "Queue at Kaldi's is 45 mins today — go tomorrow morning" "This market opens at 6am. Best produce gone by 8."

5

Engagement
Mechanics

Likes and comments are not enough. Every engagement mechanic on Hapa is designed to be location-meaningful — generating data that makes the recommendation engine smarter and rewarding users who contribute genuine local value.

📍

Been Here

Instead of liking a post about a place, users tap "Been Here" to confirm they visited. This is the most valuable signal on Hapa — it closes the loop between content and real-world action. 500 "Been Here" confirmations is evidence of real community impact that no follower count can match.

Feeds: recommendation engine, creator credibility score, business "verified popular" badge, Hapa Guide confirmation layer.
🔥

Hapa Pulse

A reaction specifically for Hapa Flash content — "This is happening right now." When enough users Pulse the same Flash post, it gets elevated into a city-wide notification. Crowdsourced real-time intelligence that no editorial team could replicate.

Feeds: Hapa Now algorithm, city-wide notifications, trending local topics.
💬

Local Comments

Comments on Hapa are location-contextualised. A comment from someone who lives in the same neighbourhood carries a "Local" badge. A comment from a verified local with high trust score carries a "Trusted Local" badge. Proximity adds credibility.

Feeds: trust scoring, community knowledge layer, search result weighting.
⬆️

Community Upvote

Used specifically on Cultural Notes and Community Ask answers. Unlike a like, an upvote means: "This is accurate, I confirm this." Community upvotes surface the most trustworthy local knowledge to the top of Hapa Guide automatically.

Feeds: Hapa Guide content ranking, cultural intelligence accuracy layer, contributor reputation score.
📌

Save to City Map

Users can save any place, post, or creator recommendation to their personal city map. When enough users save the same place, it surfaces as a "Community Favourite" in that neighbourhood. Collective curation without any editorial intervention.

Feeds: personal recommendation engine, neighbourhood trending places, Hapa Places ranking.
🏆

Local Reputation Score

Every user builds a reputation score in each city they are active in. Scored on: review accuracy ("Been Here" confirmations on their recommendations), cultural note quality (upvotes), Community Ask answer helpfulness, and general content engagement. This score is public and city-specific.

Feeds: creator application eligibility, Verified Local status, content reach multiplier, business partnership eligibility.
🎯

City Challenges

Weekly or monthly community challenges issued by Hapa or top creators. "Show us the best hidden spot in your neighbourhood." "Document the oldest business on your street." "Best street food under 2,000 UGX." A wave of coordinated local content that floods the platform with discovery.

Feeds: content volume, new user acquisition, creator visibility, cultural archive building.
👥

Hapa Circles

Interest-based community groups anchored to a city or neighbourhood. "Nairobi Food Lovers." "Kampala Nightlife." "Lagos Tech Scene." Users join circles, post within them, and discover people with shared interests who are actually nearby. The WhatsApp group — but structured, searchable, and public by default.

Feeds: people discovery, social graph building, creator audience building, targeted business discovery.
5.1 — Feed Algorithm Principles
What the Feed Prioritises
Proximity — content from within your current location always ranks first
Recency — within proximity, newer content surfaces above older
Behavioural match — content types you engage with most appear more frequently
Social graph — content from people you follow and circles you're in
Verified local signal — content from users with high local reputation scores in your city
Time context — Friday evening feed looks different from Monday morning feed
What the Feed Never Does
Never shows content from another city without explicit user request
Never prioritises paid content above genuinely high-quality organic content
Never surfaces content with high global engagement but low local relevance
Never shows the same business promotion twice in the same 24-hour window
Never replaces location context with interest context — location is always the primary filter
Never rewards low-quality content simply because it generated high reaction volume
6

Content
Moderation

Hapa is a platform rooted in real communities. Moderation must protect those communities without becoming a bureaucratic barrier to genuine local expression. The principle: fast, fair, and locally informed.

"Moderation on Hapa is not about policing content. It is about protecting the integrity of local knowledge. A false review harms a real business. A fabricated Flash post wastes real people's time. Trust is the product."

// Design Principle — Moderation
6.1 — Moderation Framework
Violation Type Detection Method First Offence Action Repeat Offence Severity
False location tag GPS verification + community flag Post removed, user warned Review posting suspended 7 days Medium
Fake business review Location gate check + report Review removed, reputation score docked Review privilege removed 30 days High
Spam / repeated promotion Frequency algorithm + reports Content suppressed from feed Account restricted Medium
Hate speech / harassment AI classifier + human review Content removed, 7-day suspension Permanent ban High
Misinformation (Flash posts) Community contradiction flags Post labelled "Unconfirmed", reach reduced Flash privileges removed Medium
Inaccurate cultural note Community downvotes Auto-deprioritised in Guide No action — community self-corrects Low
Creator paid-post without disclosure Partnership Hub check + reports Post labelled, creator warned Creator tier downgrade High
Inappropriate imagery AI classifier (immediate) Auto-removed before publish Account reviewed + suspended High
Business gaming reviews Velocity detection + cluster analysis Reviews voided, business warned Business listing suspended High
6.2 — Trust Architecture
Community-First Moderation
Any user can flag content — one tap, reason selection from a list
Three independent flags from users in the same city triggers automatic review queue
Users who flag accurately (content is later removed) build a "trusted reporter" score that gives their future flags higher priority
Users who flag maliciously (repeatedly flagging valid content) lose flagging weight over time
City Voice creators have a fast-track channel for urgent moderation issues in their city
Location Verification Layer
Reviews can only be posted by users whose GPS placed them within 500m of the business in the last 30 days
Flash posts are automatically labelled "Unverified" if the poster's location does not match the tagged location
Cultural notes are city-locked — a user who has never been to Nairobi cannot post cultural notes about Nairobi
Business location pins are cross-checked against known address databases where available
Verified Local status requires demonstrated location history in the claimed city over 60+ days

Notification
System

Notifications are Hapa's most powerful product surface. A well-timed, location-relevant push is the moment Hapa feels like magic. A poorly timed or irrelevant one is the moment users turn notifications off forever. Every notification must earn its place.

"Every notification Hapa sends should make the user think: how did it know? Not: why is it bothering me?"

// Design Principle — Notifications
7.1 — Notification Types
⚡
Hapa Now Alert — Time-Sensitive Local Event

Triggered when a Hapa Flash post reaches the "Pulse" threshold (multiple community confirmations) near the user's current location. Sent only during hours the user has been active before.

"Live music just started at Cayenne — 400m from you. 12 Hapa users there now."
📍
Predictive Discovery — Before You Ask

The signature Hapa notification. Triggered by behavioural pattern matching. User searches for food every Friday evening — Hapa sends a relevant recommendation at 6:45pm Friday before they open the app.

"It's Friday evening. New rooftop bar opened in Lekki this week — 1.2km from you. Rated 4.8 by 23 locals."
🏪
Hapa Boost — Business Promotion (Proximity-Triggered)

Sent when a registered business pushes a Hapa Boost promotion and the user is within the defined radius. Only sent once per business per day. Never more than three promotional notifications per user per day total.

"Kati Kati Kitchen — 300m away: Buy 1 get 1 free on lunch today, next 2 hours only."
🌍
Location Change — City Switch Detected

When Hapa detects the user has moved to a new city (sustained GPS data, not a brief transit). Triggers the arrival experience: cultural briefing, nearby recommendations, local creator introductions.

"You're in Nairobi. Here's what you need to know today — and what's happening tonight."
🎙️
Creator Activity — Local Creator You Follow

When a creator the user follows posts a Neighbourhood Diary, Curated Map, or Hapa Reel in the user's current city. Frequency-capped to prevent creator over-notification.

"[Creator Name] just posted a new guide to Kabalagala nightlife — 3 spots you haven't tried."
❓
Community Ask — Answer Request Near You

When a Community Ask question is posted in the user's neighbourhood and their interests or local reputation score makes them a likely good answer. Opt-in only — users choose whether to receive these.

"Someone in Kololo needs a mechanic recommendation. You've reviewed 3 mechanics nearby — can you help?"
🏆
Reputation Milestone

When a user's local reputation score crosses a new tier, a "Been Here" confirmation validates their recommendation, or their cultural note is featured in Hapa Guide. Celebrates contribution without being patronising.

"Your review of Nalongo's Kitchen just got 50 'Been Here' confirmations. You're Kampala's top-rated food reviewer this month."
7.2 — Notification Rules
Hard Limits — Non-Negotiable
Maximum 5 push notifications per user per day — total across all types
Maximum 1 promotional (Hapa Boost) notification per user per 4 hours
No notifications between 10pm and 7am local time — except user-opted emergency alerts
No notifications for a city the user has not been in for 7+ days (prevents Kampala notifications reaching a user now in Nairobi)
Notification frequency auto-reduces for users who consistently dismiss without opening — Hapa learns their tolerance
One-tap notification management — users can silence any category from the notification itself, without going into settings
8

Privacy
Architecture

Location is Hapa's most powerful asset — and its most sensitive one. Privacy architecture is not a legal obligation. It is a product decision that determines whether users trust Hapa enough to keep location on. Without that trust, there is no product.

"Hapa knows where you are to serve you — never to watch you. The moment users feel surveilled rather than served, the product fails."

// Design Principle — Privacy
8.1 — Location Data Principles

What Hapa Collects

  • Current city — for feed and recommendations
  • Precise location — only when app is open and active
  • Location history — anonymised, stored for 90 days for behaviour patterns
  • Places visited — inferred from GPS proximity to registered businesses
  • Movement patterns — time-of-day and day-of-week aggregates only

What Hapa Never Does

  • Never tracks location when app is closed (background location off by default)
  • Never shares precise location with other users — only city/neighbourhood level
  • Never sells location data to third parties
  • Never shows other users your real-time location without explicit opt-in
  • Never uses location data for purposes unrelated to the Hapa experience

User Controls

  • Ghost Mode — use Hapa without broadcasting any location signals to other users
  • Neighbourhood-only — show neighbourhood but not precise area to other users
  • Location history — view and delete your full location history at any time
  • Data export — download your full Hapa data at any time
  • Account deletion — full data purge within 30 days of request
